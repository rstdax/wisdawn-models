EXPERIMENT 03 · GRAVITATION

The Whirling Stone

What happens when the force keeping an object in a circle suddenly disappears?

I've got a stone moving in a circle. But what happens if I suddenly let go?
WHAT JUST HAPPENED (NCERT CLASS 9)

Centripetal Force & Inertia

According to the NCERT Class 9 textbook: "The force that causes this acceleration and keeps the body moving along the circular path is acting towards the centre. This force is called the centripetal (meaning ‘centre-seeking’) force."

Before Snap

Velocity→ Tangent
Centripetal Force→ Toward Center
Path→ Circular

After Snap

Velocity→ Tangent
Centripetal Force→ 0 N
Path→ Straight line

The textbook further explains: "In the absence of this force, the stone flies off along a straight line. This straight line will be a tangent to the circular path." Because the string broke, the centripetal force vanished (Fc = 0), and inertia caused the stone to maintain its tangential velocity.

FORMULA DISCOVERY

Centripetal Force Formula

As per the principles of motion (and your experiments), the magnitude of this centre-seeking force is given by the formula. Tap a term to see what it controls.

Fc = mv² / r
m — mass of the stone v — tangential velocity (squared!) r — radius of the circle

WHAT DID WE LEARN?

The Whirling Stone — Summary

🔵

Velocity is always tangential to the circular path, at every instant.

🔴

Centripetal force always points toward the centre — that's what keeps the motion circular.

🟢

When that force disappears, the object moves in a straight line along the tangent — no curve, no outward push.

📐

Centripetal force follows Fc = mv²/r — bigger with speed & mass, smaller with radius.
